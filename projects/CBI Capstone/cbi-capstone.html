CBI Rat Testing Chamber

Multidisciplinary capstone project for the CBI Lab at Ohio State, focused on designing a controlled chamber system for rat-based experiments on social interaction and chronic brain injury.

Project Overview

My team and I were hired by the CBI Lab at The Ohio State University to design and build a rat testing chamber for neuroscience experiments involving chronic brain injury. The system was designed to support controlled social interaction experiments using a multi-chamber testing environment.

Engineering Problem

The chamber needed to create a controlled testing environment where rats could be separated, observed, and selectively allowed to interact. A key challenge was designing internal chamber doors that could open and close reliably while preventing unwanted contact between animals before the test condition began.

My Role

I focused on the internal door mechanism and automation system. This included thinking through the mechanical motion of the door, material choices, actuator behavior, power and control requirements, and how the mechanism would integrate into the larger chamber.

Design Features

  • Three-chamber testing layout for controlled behavioral experiments
  • Stainless steel and plexiglass chamber construction
  • Guillotine-style internal door concept
  • Door design intended to allow visibility while preventing physical contact
  • Stepper motor and Arduino-based automation concept
  • 3D-printed mounts and mechanical support components
  • Steel bar floor grates and removable bottom drawer for cleaning and handling

Engineering Challenges

The project required balancing research needs, animal interaction constraints, manufacturability, cleaning requirements, and mechanical reliability. Fabrication precision was especially important because small alignment errors could affect door motion, fastening, and chamber assembly.

Tools & Skills Used

SolidWorks Arduino Stepper Motors Mechanical Design Prototyping Fabrication Design Documentation

Results / Lessons Learned

This project strengthened my understanding of client-driven engineering design, especially for research equipment where reliability, safety, cleaning, and repeatability matter as much as the basic mechanical function. It also showed the importance of tolerance control, fabrication planning, and designing mechanisms that can be assembled and adjusted without excessive manual correction.
